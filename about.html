THE THROUGH-LINE

Curiosity turned into a career. The career turned into a deeper engineering path.

I started in information technology in 1998 and grew through support, networking, systems administration, enterprise migrations, consulting, infrastructure architecture, field engineering, cloud, security, automation, and technical leadership. Working across those layers taught me that the hardest problems rarely belong to a single product. They live in the relationships between systems, people, processes, failure modes, and constraints.

That is why my work now crosses disciplines. I write Python and PowerShell because infrastructure should be automatable. I study software because operational systems increasingly are software. I study mathematics and physics because advanced computing rests on both. I explore AI, distributed systems, HPC, robotics, and quantum technologies because the next generation of computing will require people who can reason across hardware, software, infrastructure, and scientific models rather than treating them as isolated specialties.

I also build outside traditional infrastructure: interactive learning systems, scientific visualizations, AI experiments, automation tools, games, and browser labs. Building is how I test whether I actually understand something. If an idea cannot survive contact with implementation, debugging, measurement, and another person trying to use it, the understanding is not finished.

DIRECTION

From operating complex systems to helping design the next generation of them.

My long-term direction sits at the intersection of systems architecture, high-performance computing, distributed systems, AI, and quantum technologies. I am especially interested in the boundary between hardware and software: how compute, memory, networks, accelerators, orchestration, verification, security, and human interfaces become one usable system.

Quantum computing is part of that path, not a replacement for classical computing. The problems I care about require both: dependable classical infrastructure and software around specialized quantum capabilities, with rigorous verification and clear boundaries about what each technology can and cannot do.

Project MIND is the broadest expression of that direction: an engineering vision that combines classical AI/HPC, specialized agents, structured knowledge and verification, robotics or laboratory interfaces, and quantum computing as a specialized accelerator. The purpose is practical—accelerate learning and scientific work while keeping human judgment, evidence, and system boundaries visible.

HOW I WORK

A few principles that keep showing up.

  1. Understand the whole system.A local fix is not enough when the failure lives in a dependency.
  2. Automate the repeatable; verify the important.Automation should make work more inspectable, not more mysterious.
  3. Design for recovery.Availability is incomplete if restore, rollback, evidence, and ownership have not been considered.
  4. Build to learn.Projects, labs, experiments, and software turn theory into something testable.
  5. Make difficult ideas accessible without making them inaccurate.Good technical communication should increase understanding, not hide complexity behind jargon.
