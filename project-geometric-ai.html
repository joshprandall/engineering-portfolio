GEOMETRY / NUMERICAL EXPERIMENTS

Geometry & Physics Lab

An interactive portfolio lab for geometric features, curvature, diffusion, and Bayesian inference.

Open the working lab ↗︎View original GNP research ↗︎

What this demonstrates

Browser mathematics generates point clouds, estimates curvature, simulates an analytic sphere diffusion field, and computes a Bayesian radius posterior. Recorded pretrained Geometric Neural Operator comparisons are labeled as recorded outputs; live neural inference remains a local Python workflow.

The research foundation is the work of Blaine Quackenbush and Paul J. Atzberger. Joshua Randall’s contribution is the educational interface, experiments, notebook, integration, reproducibility tooling, and portfolio adaptation. The project makes no claim that Joshua invented the underlying architecture or improved pretrained accuracy.

AUTHORED STUDY COMPANION / 3.4

Audit the recorded geometry benchmark

Prerequisites. Curvature, regression error, data provenance and fair comparisons.

A benchmark claim needs a dataset, reference target, prediction arrays, metric and a stated information budget. This companion preserves the existing Geometry Lab and independently recomputes mean absolute errors from its six recorded sphere/torus runs. Each case contains 1,200 points; noise levels are 0, 0.01 and 0.03. The model choice, weight hashes, pinned upstream revision and historical timing definition travel with the fixture.

The GNP estimator received exact clean-surface normal orientations, while the local polynomial baseline estimated a PCA frame from coordinates. That difference supplies extra geometric information to one method and limits a direct fairness claim. Recomputing a metric confirms the arithmetic on saved arrays; it does not rerun model inference, validate unseen surfaces, or reproduce a paper's benchmark.

The clean sphere's stored GNP MAE is approximately 0.0286977 and the baseline's 0.0447102. These six examples are descriptive, with no seed ensemble or uncertainty estimate. The 2026 kernel-extension paper is a separate research reference and its methods are not implemented in the protected browser lab. A useful next study would control normal estimation, neighborhood policy and tuning budget across methods.

AUTHORED STUDY COMPANION / 3.4

Separate an exact diffusion mode from an inverse problem

Prerequisites. Differential equations, eigenfunctions, likelihoods and normalization.

On a sphere of radius r, y/r is a degree-one spherical harmonic with Laplace–Beltrami eigenvalue −2/r². Therefore u(y,t)=0.5+0.5(y/r)exp(−2Dt/r²) solves the surface heat equation for this particular initial field. The surface mean stays 0.5 and the north–south contrast decays exponentially. This analytic example is not a numerical solver for arbitrary geometry or arbitrary initial conditions.

The inverse companion uses fictional curvature observations 0.98,1.04,1.01 with independent Gaussian noise σ=0.05 and a uniform radius prior on [0.4,2.5]. A discrete radius grid receives weights proportional to exp[−Σ(Kᵢ−1/r²)²/(2σ²)]. Subtracting the largest log weight before exponentiating avoids unnecessary underflow without changing normalized probabilities.

A narrow posterior can still be wrong if observations are correlated, σ is misspecified, or the prior truncates the plausible radius. The equal-tail interval is a posterior statement under this model, not a universal guarantee about repeated measurements. Compare grids before interpreting digits beyond their spacing.
