PROJECT EVIDENCE / HOW TO READ THE WORK

Projects should show what was built, how it was tested, and where the claim stops.

This portfolio deliberately mixes production-oriented software, interactive learning systems, coursework, research-style experiments and games. Those are different evidence classes. A prototype can demonstrate architecture without proving production scale; a deterministic browser experiment can demonstrate mathematics without proving a hardware result; a passing test suite can verify the tested contract without proving the absence of every defect.

Problem

What real question, failure mode or user need makes the artifact worth building?

Artifact

What can another person actually inspect, run, download, test or play?

Method

Which architecture, algorithm, model, dataset, protocol or engineering process was used—and why?

Verification

What tests, known results, browser/device checks, fixtures, invariants or reproducibility steps support the stated behavior?

Limitations

What has not been validated, what assumptions constrain the result, and what would break the conclusion?

Next experiment

What measurable change would most improve confidence, usefulness, performance or scientific value?
