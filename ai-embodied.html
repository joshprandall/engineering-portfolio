What it is

Connect perception, state estimation, planning, and feedback control to physical or simulated action while treating latency, uncertainty, and safety as first-class constraints.

This page separates the concept, implementation path, interactive experiment, evaluation criteria, failure modes, and sources so the topic can be studied without relying on a summary tile.

Core concepts

  • Sensors provide noisy partial observations of the environment.
  • State estimation converts observations into a usable belief about position, velocity, objects, or environment state.
  • Planning chooses a path or action sequence.
  • Controllers turn target states into actuator commands.
  • Safety systems bound what actions can occur even when perception or planning is wrong.

Architecture / workflow

  1. Model the robot, sensors, actuators, limits, and environment.
  2. Validate perception/state estimation separately from control.
  3. Test controllers in simulation with noise and latency.
  4. Add planning and obstacle handling.
  5. Move to hardware only with explicit speed/force limits, stop mechanisms, logging, and staged validation.

Technical foundations

  • Proportional control: u=Kₚ(r−y), often clamped by actuator limits.
  • PID control adds integral and derivative terms for accumulated error and damping.
  • Kinematics relate joint variables to position/orientation; dynamics add forces and inertia.
  • State estimators such as Kalman filters combine a motion model with noisy measurements.

Evaluation checklist

  • Tracking error and settling time.
  • Collision/constraint violations.
  • Robustness to sensor noise, dropped observations, and latency.
  • Recovery after blocked paths or actuator saturation.
  • Simulation-to-real gap measured on the actual hardware.

Failure modes and limits

  • A controller stable in simulation can be unsafe on hardware.
  • Perception errors can become motion errors.
  • Latency can destabilize feedback loops.
  • Mechanical limits, friction, wear, and unmodeled dynamics matter.

Practical build path

  1. Simulate a one-dimensional controller with noise.
  2. Add saturation and latency and inspect stability.
  3. Add a simple planner around obstacles.
  4. Transfer only after defining hardware limits and emergency-stop behavior.
